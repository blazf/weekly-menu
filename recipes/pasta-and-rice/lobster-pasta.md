Bucatini in a lobster-stock cream sauce.

## Ingredients

- 2 (1½ lb) live lobsters
- 1 medium onion, coarsely chopped
- 2 celery stalks, trimmed and coarsely chopped
- 2 sprigs basil or tarragon
- 3 tbsp chopped chives
- 1 lb bucatini or spaghetti
- 3 oz Parmesan, finely grated (about 1 cup), plus more for serving
- 4 cups heavy cream
- 1 tbsp unsalted butter
- ½ tsp crushed red pepper flakes
- 3 garlic cloves, peeled and smashed
- Kosher salt
- Freshly ground black pepper

## Method

1. Bring a large pot of heavily salted water to a boil. Add the lobsters and cook **8–10 minutes**, until the shells are bright red. Transfer to a bowl of ice water to stop the cooking.
2. Pick the meat from the tails, claws and knuckles. Cut into bite-size pieces and refrigerate. **Keep the shells and bodies** — they carry most of the flavour.
3. Melt the butter in a large pot over medium heat. Add the crushed shells, onion and celery and cook **5 minutes**, until fragrant.
4. Add the basil or tarragon and enough water to just cover, about 6 cups. Simmer **30 minutes**, then strain and discard the solids.
5. Return the stock to the pot and boil until reduced to about **1 cup**.
6. In a wide skillet over medium-low heat, warm the garlic and red pepper flakes until fragrant, about **1 minute**. Add the cream and the reduced stock and simmer **15–20 minutes**, until reduced by roughly half and thick enough to coat a spoon. Season with salt and pepper, and fish out the garlic cloves.
7. Meanwhile, cook the bucatini in salted water until just shy of al dente. **Reserve a cup of pasta water** before draining.
8. Toss the pasta into the sauce with the Parmesan, loosening with pasta water until it coats every strand. Add the lobster meat and warm through **1–2 minutes** — no longer, or it turns rubbery.
9. Off the heat, stir in the chives. Serve with more Parmesan and black pepper.

## Notes

- The lobster meat goes in at the very end. It is already cooked from step 1, and reheating it hard is the one way to ruin this dish.
- If the sauce tightens while the pasta finishes, loosen it with a splash of pasta water rather than more cream.
