# Gnocchi

Light, pillowy potato gnocchi depend on one rule: minimize moisture and handle the dough as gently as possible. Excess water or heavy kneading forces you to add too much flour, turning light dumplings dense and chewy.

## Ingredients

- 2 lb (900 g) Russet or starchy yellow potatoes
- 1 cup (130 g) all-purpose or Type "00" flour, plus extra for dusting
- 1 large egg yolk, or 1 whole egg lightly beaten
- 1 tsp fine sea salt
- A pinch of ground nutmeg

## Method

1. Preheat the oven to **400°F (200°C)**. Pierce the potatoes several times with a fork and bake whole for **45–55 minutes**, until tender to the center. Dry heat prevents waterlogged dough. Cut them open immediately and let the steam escape for **5 minutes**.
2. Peel the skins away while the potatoes are still warm. Pass the flesh through a ricer or food mill directly onto a clean work surface in an even, airy layer. Never mash or blend — it makes them gummy. Let cool **3–4 minutes**.
3. Sprinkle the salt, nutmeg and about ¾ of the flour over the riced potatoes. Pour the egg yolk into the center. Use a bench scraper to gently cut and fold everything together, pressing lightly until a soft dough forms. Add the remaining flour only if the dough is visibly sticky — over-kneading makes it rubbery.
4. Divide the dough into 4 pieces. Dust your hands and surface with flour and roll each piece into a rope about ¾ inch (2 cm) thick. Cut into ¾-inch pillows and roll each with light thumb pressure over fork tines or a gnocchi board to create ridges.
5. Bring a large pot of salted water to a gentle boil. Drop the gnocchi in batches so they do not crowd. Once they float, cook another **60–90 seconds**, then lift them with a spider strainer directly into a warm skillet with your sauce.

## Notes

- Browned butter with crisp sage is the classic pairing.
