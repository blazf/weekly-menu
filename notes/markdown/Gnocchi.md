# Gnocchi  
  
Light, pillowy potato gnocchi depend on one golden rule: **minimize moisture and handle the dough as gently as possible**. Excess water or heavy kneading forces you to add too much flour, turning light dumplings dense and chewy.  
  
**Ingredients (Serves 4)**  
* **Potatoes:** 2 lbs (approx. 900g) Russet or starchy yellow potatoes  
* **Flour:** 1 cup (approx. 130g) all-purpose or Italian Type "00" flour, plus extra for dusting  
* **Egg:** 1 large egg yolk (or 1 whole egg, lightly beaten)  
* **Seasoning:** 1 tsp fine sea salt and a pinch of ground nutmeg  
**1.Bake and Steam the Potatoes:**Dry heat prevents waterlogged dough.  
Preheat your oven to 400°F (200°C). Pierce potatoes several times with a fork and bake whole on a baking sheet for 45–55 minutes until completely tender to the center. Cut them open immediately to let trapped steam escape for 5 minutes.  
**2.Rice While Warm:**Never mash or blend, which makes potatoes gummy.  
Peel the skins away while the potatoes are still warm. Pass the flesh through a potato ricer or food mill directly onto a clean work surface in an even, airy layer. Let it cool for 3–4 minutes until steam subsides.  
**3.Gently Bring Dough Together:**Over-kneading creates dense, rubbery texture.  
Sprinkle the salt, nutmeg, and about 3/4 of the flour evenly over the riced potatoes. Pour the egg yolk in the center. Use a bench scraper to gently cut and fold the ingredients together, pressing lightly until a soft, smooth dough forms. Only add the remaining flour if the dough is visibly sticky.  
**4.Roll, Cut, and Shape:**  
Divide the dough into 4 equal pieces. Lightly dust your hands and surface with flour, then roll each piece into a uniform rope about 3/4-inch (2 cm) thick. Cut into 3/4-inch pillows using your bench scraper. Roll each piece with light thumb pressure over the tines of a fork or a gnocchi board to create ridges.  
**5.Boil and Sauce Directly:**  
Bring a large pot of salted water to a gentle boil. Drop the gnocchi in batches so they do not crowd. Once they float to the surface, cook for another 60–90 seconds, then transfer them with a spider strainer directly into a warm skillet with your sauce (such as browned butter and crisp sage).
